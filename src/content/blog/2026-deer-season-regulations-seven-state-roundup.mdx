**2026 deer season regulations** changed in every one of these seven states — a new zone system in Iowa, a shortened muzzleloader season in Michigan, a permanent crossbow rule in Minnesota. None of it touches which cartridge you can carry; we already covered that state by state in our [straight-wall cartridge regulations guide](/blog/straight-wall-cartridge-state-regulations), part of our broader [SWC hunting hub](/blog/ultimate-guide-straight-wall-cartridge-hunting). This one covers the other half: season dates, bag limits, and one-buck rules.

Every state below revises its regulations booklet at least once a year, and county- or DMU-level exceptions can override anything listed here. Treat this as a starting point, not a substitute for checking your state DNR's current-year regulations before you buy a license or head to the field.

## The short version: what changed for 2026

- **Iowa** split resident deer hunting into two zones for the first time. Eight northwest counties are now antlered-buck-only in most seasons.
- **Michigan** cut its muzzleloader season from 10 days to 3 and opened it to any legal firearm in the Lower Peninsula. Its true one-buck rule does **not** start until 2027 — for 2026, the statewide limit is still two antlered deer per hunter.
- **Wisconsin** removed the sunset on crossbow use — any archery license now covers a crossbow for the entire season, permanently.
- **Minnesota** confirms its statewide one-buck-per-year cap applies no matter how many seasons or weapons you hunt with.
- **Illinois, Indiana, and Ohio** kept their core structures the same, with county-level bag limit tweaks.

## Ohio: steady as it goes, with county-level tweaks

Ohio's structure is nearly identical to 2025-26. Hunting hours remain 30 minutes before sunrise to 30 minutes after sunset.

**Only one antlered deer may be harvested per hunter, regardless of where or how it's taken** — Ohio's one-buck rule has been in place for years.

Archery runs Sept. 26, 2026 through Feb. 7, 2027. Youth gun weekend is Nov. 21–22. Gun week runs Nov. 30–Dec. 6, with a bonus weekend Dec. 19–20. Muzzleloader runs Jan. 2–5, 2027.

Bag limits are county-based and shifted this year for disease reasons: Athens, Meigs, and Washington counties — hit by an EHD outbreak in 2025 — are down to two deer (max one antlerless), while Defiance, Paulding, and Warren counties got bumped up to three.

## Michigan: shorter muzzleloader season, one-buck rule delayed to 2027

Michigan made the most changes of any state on this list for 2026, and the headline one — the one-buck rule — **isn't actually live yet**.

What's live: the muzzleloader season shrinks from 10 days to 3, starting the first Friday in December (Dec. 4–6, 2026), renamed the "December Firearm Deer Season," with any legal firearm now allowed during it in the Lower Peninsula. Late Antlerless Season opens the following Monday (Dec. 7) and runs through Jan. 1. The Limited Firearms Deer Zone in the Lower Peninsula is gone entirely — bottleneck cartridges are legal statewide there now.

The one-buck rule the NRC approved applies to the Lower Peninsula starting with the **2027** season, not 2026. For 2026, Michigan's actual statewide limit is **two antlered deer per hunter per year** (DMU 117 is the one exception, limited to one). A single deer license covers one buck (3+ points on one side) or one antlerless deer; a combo license covers one antlered plus one antlerless, or two antlerless — and a hunter can hold both license types in the same year, which is how the two-buck total is reached.

## Indiana: unchanged bag limit, still the strictest of the seven

Indiana kept the 2025-26 overhaul in place for 2026-27. Archery runs Oct. 1, 2026 through Jan. 3, 2027. Firearms season is Nov. 14–29. Muzzleloader runs Dec. 5–20. Youth season is Sept. 26–27.

The bag limit is the most clear-cut on this list: **one antlered deer and six antlerless deer, combined, across youth, archery, firearms, and muzzleloader seasons.** County antlerless quotas can cap you below the statewide six. Deer Reduction Zones run separately (Sept. 15, 2026 – Jan. 31, 2027) with a 10-deer limit and an earn-a-buck requirement.

## Iowa: a brand-new zone system for 2026

Iowa's headline change didn't touch dates — it touched **who can shoot what, and where**. Starting this season, Iowa splits resident deer hunting into **Zone A** and **Zone B**.

Zone B covers eight northwest counties: Crawford, Ida, Lyon, O'Brien, Plymouth, Shelby, Sioux, and Woodbury. Hunting there is **antlered-buck-only** in every season except Youth, Disabled, Landowner-Tenant, and Depredation. A limited number of county-specific Any-Deer licenses go on sale Aug. 15, first-come, first-served. Party hunting isn't allowed in Zone B.

Zone A is everywhere else, mostly unchanged, though Audubon, Carroll, and Greene counties were just added to the antlered-buck-only restriction during first regular gun season.

Regular Gun 1 runs Dec. 5–9, 2026; Regular Gun 2 runs Dec. 12–20. Archery/muzzleloader share a split window: Oct. 1–Dec. 4, then Dec. 21–Jan. 10, 2027. Iowa doesn't use a one-buck framework — resident hunters can hold up to two General Deer licenses (one archery, one firearm season), each good for one deer, with Zone B restricting whether that tag is any-sex or antlered-only.

*Iowa's 2026-27 youth/disabled season dates weren't confirmed in the primary sources available at the time of writing — verify at iowadnr.gov before planning around them.*

## Illinois: two-buck cap, lottery-driven firearm permits

Illinois runs on a permit-lottery system rather than an open license. Archery runs Oct. 1, 2026 through Jan. 18, 2027 (closed in counties during their local firearm segments). Firearm season splits into two segments: Nov. 20–22 and Dec. 3–6, 2026. Remaining firearm/muzzleloader permits go on sale over the counter starting Oct. 20, 2026 once lottery quotas are filled.

Illinois' bag limit works differently than most of its neighbors: there's **no statewide numeric limit on total deer**, but hunters are capped at **two antlered bucks per year across all seasons combined** (a Special CWD season in select counties, Jan. 1–4 and Jan. 15–17, 2027, is the one exception that allows additional antlered deer).

*Muzzleloader-only season and exact youth-hunt dates for 2026 weren't confirmed via a DNR-published primary source at the time of writing — Illinois' regulations booklet typically finalizes closer to the season, so confirm both before you plan around them.*

## Minnesota: one buck, one year, no matter how you hunt

Minnesota's core rule is simple and strict: **one buck per license year, statewide, regardless of how many seasons or weapons you use.** If you tag a buck during archery season, that's your buck for the year — firearms and muzzleloader seasons won't give you another shot at one unless you draw a special permit.

Antlerless harvest is where Minnesota gets complicated: it's managed through Deer Permit Areas (DPAs), each designated bucks-only, lottery, either-sex, or a 2-, 3-, or 5-deer limit, with a statewide antlerless cap of five.

Archery opens statewide Sept. 19, 2026. The firearms opener falls Nov. 7, with season length varying 9–16 days depending on your DPA's series. Muzzleloader runs statewide Nov. 28–Dec. 13. One notable 2026 change: the legislature made Minnesota's crossbow-for-all-archery-hunters rule permanent — no more sunset date, no special permit needed.

## Wisconsin: no antler point restriction, but tags don't carry across seasons

Wisconsin is the loosest of the seven on total buck limits — there's no statewide antler point restriction and no one-buck rule. The bag limit is one buck deer per unused buck harvest authorization (valid statewide) and one antlerless deer per unused antlerless authorization (valid for a specific zone/DMU/land type). Buy more authorizations, tag more deer, subject to zone-specific antlerless availability.

One detail that trips people up: buck harvest authorizations are **season- and weapon-specific**. A bow buck tag from archery season doesn't carry over to gun season — if you fill it in October, you don't get another crack at a buck in November unless you've separately bought a gun-season buck authorization.

Archery and crossbow season runs Sept. 12, 2026 through Jan. 3, 2027 statewide (through Jan. 31, 2027 in Metro subunits and select DMUs). The youth hunt is Oct. 10–11 statewide (some sponsored properties open Oct. 3). The traditional 9-day gun season runs Nov. 21–29, 2026, immediately followed by a 10-day muzzleloader season, Nov. 30–Dec. 9. A statewide Antlerless-Only Holiday Hunt runs Dec. 24–Jan. 1, 2027.

## FAQ

### Which of these seven states have a true one-buck rule right now?

Ohio, Indiana, and Minnesota do. Michigan allows two antlered deer per hunter per year statewide (one in DMU 117) until its true one-buck rule starts in 2027. Iowa, Illinois, and Wisconsin all use different systems — license-count caps, a two-buck annual limit, or season-specific harvest authorizations — instead of a strict one-buck framework.

### Why is Michigan's one-buck rule confusing people this year?

It was approved in the same announcement as several other 2026 changes, so it's easy to assume it's already active. It isn't — it applies to the Lower Peninsula starting with the 2027 season. For 2026, Michigan's statewide limit is still two antlered deer per hunter (one in DMU 117).

### Does a Wisconsin buck tag carry over between archery and gun season?

No. Wisconsin's buck harvest authorizations are season- and weapon-specific — filling a bow tag in archery season doesn't give you a buck opportunity in gun season unless you've separately purchased a gun-season buck authorization.

### What's the biggest structural change for 2026?

Iowa's new Zone A/Zone B system — the first time the state has divided resident deer licensing by zone rather than county-by-county antlerless quotas alone.
