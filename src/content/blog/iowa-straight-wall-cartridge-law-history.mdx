Iowa's first shotgun season opens Saturday, Dec. 5. If you're carrying a .350 Legend, you're using a rule that's less than ten years old. The Iowa straight wall cartridge law didn't take its current form until 2021, and that surprises a lot of hunters.

Plenty of folks figure Iowa "just" opened up to rifles. It didn't. It happened in steps over about five years, and the middle step matters most. Here's the timeline, the rule as it reads today, and what Iowa hunters told the DNR about going further.

## Step One, 2017: Rifles Get a Foot in the Door

Before 2017, a rifle wasn't a legal way to take a deer in Iowa's regular gun seasons. House File 475 changed that. The Legislature passed it without dissent, and Gov. Terry Branstad signed it April 13, 2017. The rule that put it to work took effect July 12.

The DNR said the point was a lower-recoil option for older and younger hunters. But it wasn't limited to them. Anyone with a deer license could use it in four seasons: youth, disabled hunter, and both shotgun seasons.

The catch was the cartridge. A rifle had to shoot straight-wall ammo already approved for handguns, .357 caliber or bigger with an expanding bullet. Three cartridges, the .375 Winchester, .444 Marlin and .45-70 Government, had already been legal in handguns, so the DNR grandfathered them in. The DNR told hunters that some of the approved cartridges would be effective out to about 100 yards, and most to around 50.

Not everyone liked it. The state logged 43 comments on the rule package, and 25 were against straight-wall rifles. Seven backed them outright. Five more wanted conditions, like a limit on how many rounds a rifle could hold.

## Step Two, 2020 and 2021: Iowa Drops the Shape Rule

This is the change that's easy to miss. House File 716 passed the House in March 2019. Gov. Kim Reynolds signed it June 18, 2020.

HF 716 rewrote what counts as a legal deer rifle. The statute stopped saying "straight wall" and started describing the bullet: expanding type, between .350 and .500 inch across, with at least 500 foot-pounds of muzzle energy. Think of a fence law. The old rule said what the fence had to look like. The new rule says how high it has to be.

The rules didn't catch up right away. In December 2020, the DNR asked hunters to weigh in on a proposed list of 46 approved cartridges. It left out eight that it called ballistically unlike approved handgun rounds, including the .35 Whelen, the .45-70 Government and the .444 Marlin. The last two had been legal in rifles since 2017.

The rule adopted April 8, 2021, and effective June 9, tied Chapter 106 to the numbers in the statute. Of seven public comments, five backed letting rifles in, including .45-70 and .444 calibers. By that fall, the DNR's gun season preview said it plainly:

- Hunters are no longer limited to straight-walled cartridge rifles.
- The .350 Legend is legal, and so is the .45-70.
- The list of example cartridges isn't complete. Bullet diameter is the test.

So 2021 was the real turning point. It was the first season a bottleneck cartridge, like the .35 Whelen, could be legal in the Iowa shotgun seasons.

## The Iowa Rifle Rule Today

For youth, disabled hunter, and both shotgun seasons (Dec. 5 to 9 and Dec. 12 to 20 this year), the Iowa DNR's rifle rule comes down to three numbers:

- **Bullet type:** expanding. No full-metal-jacket.
- **Bullet diameter:** at least .350 inch and no more than .500 inch.
- **Muzzle energy:** at least 500 foot-pounds, published or calculated.

Straight-wall and necked-down cartridges both qualify. The DNR's own examples include the .350 Legend, .450 Bushmaster, .45-70 Government, .35 Whelen and .444 Marlin, and it says the list isn't exhaustive.

What's out is anything with a smaller bullet. A .308 Winchester or .30-06 shoots a .308-inch bullet, which is under the .350 floor. Getting that wrong isn't a slap on the wrist. Having a prohibited rifle while deer hunting is a scheduled violation, and a conviction costs you your hunting privileges for two years.

Rifles aren't legal in archery or the early muzzleloader season, and the late muzzleloader season allows handguns but not cartridge rifles. For loads that fit the rule, see our [Iowa .350 and .400 Legend loads guide](/blog/best-legend-loads-iowa-gun-seasons/).

## The Second Door: January Antlerless Rifles

In 2022, lawmakers opened a separate rifle door. Senate File 581, signed June 17, 2022, set up a rifle-only January antlerless season for counties with unsold antlerless tags.

Under Iowa Code 483A.8, a rifle in that season needs a barrel of at least 16 inches, centerfire ammo, an expanding bullet between .223 and .500 inch, and 500 foot-pounds of muzzle energy. So in that season, the floor drops from .350 to .223.

For 2026-27, the Population Management January season is limited to Allamakee and Winneshiek counties, and only if more than 100 antlerless licenses are unsold on the third Monday in December. The Excess Tag season runs Jan. 11 to 24, 2027, in counties with leftover tags.

## What Iowa Hunters Told the DNR About Going Further

The DNR's 2023 deer hunter survey went to 16,501 hunters, and 4,062 answered, about 25 percent. One section asked about "additional rifles" during the shotgun seasons. It didn't name calibers, and it wasn't a vote. But it shows where hunters split.

- **52 percent** agreed the additional firearms would be a more effective method of harvest. **22 percent** disagreed.
- **67 percent** agreed high-powered rifles in special seasons in CWD-endemic areas would allow better disease management. **12 percent** disagreed.
- **52 percent** agreed the long-range capabilities would create an unsafe hunting environment. **27 percent** disagreed.
- **43 percent** agreed it would erode the traditions of the shotgun seasons. **30 percent** disagreed.

So hunters are split. Many see the benefit, and just as many worry about safety. Sixty-nine percent said they already own a high-powered rifle.

Any further change would run through the Legislature. In 2021, the DNR said changing allowable cartridges takes a change in Iowa law. Its July 2026 rundown of changes for this season doesn't mention rifles.

## What This Means on a Row-Crop Field Edge

The history explains why Iowa's rule reads the way it does. It was built in pieces, starting with a short-range, low-recoil option and widened by statute, not by the DNR on its own.

For you in the stand, it boils down to a few checks:

- **Check the bullet, not the brass.** Diameter, expanding type and energy are what the law measures. [Our ballistics tool](/ballistics-comparison/) shows how a .350 Legend stacks up against other cartridges.
- **Know your season.** Cartridge rifles are legal in youth, disabled hunter and both shotgun seasons, plus the January antlerless rifle seasons under their own numbers.
- **Don't assume an old article is current.** If a post tells you Iowa is "straight-wall only," it's pre-2021. Our [straight-wall cartridge regulations guide](/blog/straight-wall-cartridge-state-regulations/) and [Iowa season dates page](/iowa-deer-season-dates/) are updated yearly.
- **When in doubt, ask.** The DNR tells hunters to check with a local conservation officer if they're unsure about a cartridge.

## FAQ: The Iowa Straight Wall Cartridge Law

### Is Iowa a straight-wall cartridge state?

Not by case shape. Since the 2021 season, Iowa's test is the bullet: expanding type, between .350 and .500 inch across, with at least 500 foot-pounds of muzzle energy. Straight-wall and necked-down cartridges both qualify, in the youth, disabled hunter and both shotgun seasons.

### When did Iowa allow rifles for deer hunting?

Iowa allowed straight-wall cartridge rifles starting with the 2017 seasons. House File 475 was signed April 13, 2017, and the rule took effect July 12, 2017. The shape requirement went away with House File 716, signed June 18, 2020, and the rules caught up for the 2021 season.

### Can I use a .308 or .30-06 in Iowa's shotgun seasons?

No. Those cartridges shoot a .308-inch bullet, which is under Iowa's .350-inch minimum. The only exception is the January antlerless seasons, where the minimum bullet diameter is .223 inch and only rifles are allowed.

### Is the .45-70 legal for deer in Iowa?

Yes. It was on the DNR's list of cartridges allowed in rifles in 2017, the DNR confirmed it in its 2021 gun season preview, and it's on the DNR's current list of example cartridges.

### Could Iowa's rifle rules change again?

The DNR said in 2021 that changing allowable cartridges would take a change in Iowa law through the Legislature. Its July 2026 list of changes for this season doesn't mention rifles or cartridges.

## The Bottom Line

Iowa didn't flip a switch. It opened the door to straight-wall rifles in 2017, then in 2021 swapped the shape rule for a bullet-size-and-energy test. If your rifle's bullet is .350 to .500 inch, expanding, and carries 500 foot-pounds, you're in. If it's a .308, you're not, no matter how good a deer rifle it is.
