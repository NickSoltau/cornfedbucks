The best .350 and .400 Legend loads for Iowa deer hunting come down to two things: an expanding bullet, and using it in the right season. Any factory soft-point or polymer-tip Legend hunting load is legal in Iowa in its seasons. Iowa's rifle rule asks for an expanding bullet between .350 and .500 inch, with at least 500 foot-pounds of muzzle energy. Every hunting load we checked clears that energy line by a wide margin. The part that trips people up is the calendar. Iowa lets you use a cartridge rifle in only three windows, and the muzzleloader seasons aren't one of them.

This guide covers the rule, the season dates, and how the published numbers compare for the loads we track. It isn't a test. The figures are the manufacturers' own, and where a number came from a source we couldn't fully confirm, we left it out.

## Where You Can Use a Legend Rifle in Iowa

Iowa Code 481A.48 lets a hunter use a cartridge rifle in the Youth and Disabled seasons and in the first and second shotgun seasons. It doesn't list the early or late muzzleloader seasons, or archery. That's why we found no "overlap" between a Legend rifle and a muzzleloader hunt. The two are separate seasons with separate legal weapons.

For 2026-27, our [Iowa deer season dates page](/iowa-deer-season-dates/) has these windows:

- **Youth and Disabled:** Sept. 19 to Oct. 4, 2026.
- **First gun season:** Dec. 5 to 9, 2026.
- **Second gun season:** Dec. 12 to 20, 2026.

That's 14 days of regular gun season for a rifle hunter. The late muzzleloader season (Dec. 21 to Jan. 10) is for muzzleloaders, handguns, crossbows, and bows.

Iowa's January antlerless seasons are a smaller exception. In a few counties with unsold tags, centerfire rifles are allowed. The caliber floor has been reported as .223 in recent DNR coverage, but we saw it written more than one way, so check the current DNR booklet before you plan around it.

**Zone B doesn't change the load.** In the eight northwest Zone B counties, a general license is antlered-buck-only and party hunting isn't allowed. Those limits apply to what you shoot, not what you shoot it with.

## What Makes a Load Legal in Iowa

There are three tests.

- **Bullet diameter of .350 to .500 inch.** The .350 Legend uses a .357-inch bullet and the .400 Legend uses a .4005-inch bullet, so both pass.
- **An expanding-type bullet.** Soft points, polymer tips, bonded soft points, and all-copper hunting bullets are built to expand. Full-metal-jacket range ammo is not. Don't buy the cheap range box and carry it into the field.
- **At least 500 foot-pounds of muzzle energy.** Iowa's rule says published or calculated.

The energy test is the easy one. The weakest factory load below still starts at about 3.4 times the minimum. The expanding-bullet test is where hunters can make a mistake, because a box can say ".350 Legend" and still be a target load.

## How to Pick for Iowa's Two Gun Seasons

This section is our reading of the numbers, not a test result.

- **Timber sits and thick cover:** pick the heavier bullet. A 180-grain .350 or a 215-grain .400 has more weight behind it on a marginal angle, and your shots will be short anyway.
- **Field edges and longer shots:** pick the flatter load. The Federal Fusion in .350 and the Deer Season XP in .400 have the flattest published drops of their groups.
- **Recoil:** the .350 Legend is the milder of the two. If you're shooting a lot of practice before December, that matters. The rule doesn't favor either one.
- **Lead-free:** the Winchester Copper Impact and Barnes VOR-TX are the all-copper options in our .350 list.

Whatever you choose, shoot it before the season. Group it at 100 yards, then check where it hits at 150 or 200. A load that doesn't group in your rifle isn't the right load, whatever the table says. Our [ballistics comparison tool](/ballistics-comparison/) plots these same loads side by side.

## What Won't Pass

- **Full-metal-jacket and other non-expanding loads.** Iowa requires an expanding-type bullet. FMJ range loads, such as the 145-grain Winchester USA load sold in .350 Legend, aren't built to expand and don't qualify.
- **A subsonic suppressed load, as a hunting round.** Winchester's .400 Legend Super Suppressed is a 300-grain open-tip load at roughly 1,000 fps (sources list it between 983 and 1,060). By our math that's well over 500 ft-lbs, so it clears the energy line. Whether its bullet counts as expanding-type at that speed is a question for the DNR or a conservation officer, and we couldn't confirm it. It's built for suppressed shooting, not as a deer load.
- **Any rifle outside its three windows.** The best load in the world is still illegal in the early muzzleloader season.

Rules change. Iowa's rifle standard has changed more than once, and our [straight-wall cartridge regulations guide](/blog/straight-wall-cartridge-state-regulations/) tracks how Iowa compares to the other six states. Check the current DNR booklet before you buy ammo.

## FAQ: Legend Loads in Iowa

### Can I use a .350 Legend in Iowa's muzzleloader season?

No. Iowa Code 481A.48 allows cartridge rifles in the Youth and Disabled seasons and the first and second shotgun seasons. The early and late muzzleloader seasons are for muzzleloaders, and the late season also allows handguns, crossbows, and bows.

### Is the .350 Legend legal for deer in Iowa?

Yes, in its seasons. Iowa requires an expanding-type bullet between .350 and .500 inch, with at least 500 foot-pounds of muzzle energy. The .350 Legend uses a .357-inch bullet, and every factory hunting load we checked starts above 1,700 ft-lbs.

### Do I need a straight-wall cartridge to hunt deer in Iowa?

No. Iowa dropped the case-shape rule. Straight-wall or necked-down cartridges qualify if the bullet is expanding, between .350 and .500 inch, and meets the energy minimum. A .350 or .400 Legend is a good fit for most Iowa shots, but it isn't required.

## The Bottom Line

Iowa's rifle rule is easy to meet and easy to misread. Buy a soft-point or polymer-tip Legend load, and the diameter, bullet, and energy tests are covered. Then plan around the calendar: 14 days of regular gun season, plus the Youth and Disabled window, and no rifle in the muzzleloader seasons. If you're still choosing between the two cartridges, start with the [.350 vs. .400 comparison](/blog/350-legend-vs-400-legend/).
