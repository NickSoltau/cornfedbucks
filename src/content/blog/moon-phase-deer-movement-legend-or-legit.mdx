**New series: Legend or Legit.** Every deer camp has a handful of "rules" that get repeated so often nobody questions them anymore. Some hold up. Some don't. Each entry in this series takes one claim, digs into what the actual research says, and gives you a straight verdict — not a hedge, not "it depends." First up: the moon.

## The Claim

You've heard it, and you've probably planned a sit around it: deer move more on certain moon phases, certain moon positions overhead, or during a "solunar window" some app or calendar printed out for you. A full moon means deer feed all night and go quiet by daylight. A new moon means big daytime movement because they "have to" feed during the day. Whole apps and paid calendars exist to tell you which days to burn vacation time on.

It's a tidy theory. The question is whether it's actually true.

## What the Research Found

Mississippi State University's Deer Lab put this to an actual test instead of a guess. Researchers GPS-collared 48 bucks — many of them mature — across two full hunting seasons in central Mississippi, pinging their location every 15 minutes from September through February. That's enough data to see real patterns, not anecdotes from one good sit.

The result: moon phase and moon position didn't move the needle. On what solunar theory would call the "best" moon days, hourly movement ticked up by about 4 yards — a difference small enough to fall inside the GPS unit's own margin of error. MSU deer biologist Dr. Bronson Strickland called the effect "trivial at best," and the team's own conclusion was blunt: there's no conclusive data linking moon phase or position to buck movement.

It's not just one study, either. The Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries, and Parks points to the same finding and notes it lines up with separate research out of Penn State, Texas, North Carolina, and the Southeastern Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies — different states, different deer herds, same answer. Every one of them found deer keying on the rut, time of day, weather, and hunting pressure, not the moon.

The gap between that finding and what hunters actually believe is the real story here: a Mississippi survey found 83% of hunters there think the moon strongly affects deer behavior. That's not a fringe opinion — that's most of deer camp, running a hunting decision on a variable the data says isn't driving anything.

## The Verdict: Legend

This one doesn't hold up. Moon phase is not a meaningful driver of deer movement. The wobble that shows up in the raw numbers on certain phases is closer to statistical noise than a pattern you can hunt around — and it's dwarfed by factors that actually do move deer: the rut kicking in, a cold front, a neighbor pushing a field, or just the last hour of daylight doing what it always does.

That's a separate question from whether the moon affects *when* the rut happens — [it doesn't, and we've already covered that one](/blog/when-does-the-rut-start). This piece is about day-to-day movement volume, not breeding timing, and the two lines of research agree: whatever small shift shows up around certain moon phases isn't the lever hunters think it is, on either front.

## What to Actually Do With This

- **Stop building sits around solunar windows.** If a "major" or "minor" period lines up with dawn or dusk, you were already going to hunt that window anyway — the moon isn't adding anything on top of it.
- **Weight the factors the research actually backs.** Rut timing, a fresh cold front, wind direction, and hunting pressure all move deer more than any moon phase does. Spend your planning time there.
- **Don't let a "bad moon day" talk you out of a good sit.** If the wind's right, the food source is fresh, and you've got the time — go. The data says the moon isn't the reason to stay home.

## FAQ: Moon Phase and Deer Movement

### Does the moon phase affect how much deer move?

Not meaningfully. A GPS-collar study on 48 bucks over two hunting seasons found the movement difference between moon phases was small enough to fall inside the tracking equipment's own margin of error — described by the researchers as "trivial at best."

### Does the moon affect when the rut starts?

No. That's a separate, already-settled question — moon phase doesn't shift peak breeding timing, which runs on photoperiod instead.

### What actually does drive deer movement, if not the moon?

The rut, time of day, weather (especially cooling temperatures and approaching fronts), and hunting pressure all showed a far larger effect on deer activity than moon phase or position did in the research.

### Should I stop using a solunar calendar or app?

You don't have to throw it out, but don't build a hunt plan around it. The research doesn't support solunar windows as a reliable predictor of deer activity — factors like wind, temperature, and time of day are worth far more of your attention.

## The Bottom Line

Moon phase feels like it should matter — it's a clean, simple variable that's easy to plan around. But 48 GPS-collared bucks and corroborating research from four other states say it isn't doing what deer camp thinks it's doing. File this one under Legend, put the planning time you'd spend checking a solunar app into checking the wind and the weather instead, and hunt the sits that are actually good — moon be damned.
