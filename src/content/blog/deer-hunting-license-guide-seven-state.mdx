**A resident deer license across the seven-state Corn Belt tier runs anywhere from about $24 (Wisconsin, deer license alone) to $59 (Indiana) once tags and fees are added up. Nonresidents pay far more — Wisconsin's nonresident deer license is a flat $200 regardless of weapon, confirmed directly against the state's own fee tables. Iowa and Illinois cap tags through a lottery instead of selling them over the counter.** This guide breaks down what you'll pay, when each season opens and closes, and how you'll actually get your tag in Ohio, Michigan, Indiana, Iowa, Illinois, Minnesota, and Wisconsin for the 2026-27 season — pulled straight from each state's DNR, not an aggregator's guess.

We built this because the numbers and dates floating around online for these states are a mess. Search "Ohio deer license cost" and you'll find one site claiming $18, another claiming $174 for a nonresident, and a third splitting the difference. Season dates get mangled just as often — last year's dates copy-pasted forward, or a neighboring state's zone structure applied where it doesn't belong. Below are the actual 2026-27 figures and dates, sourced directly from ODNR, IN.gov, IowaDNR.gov, IDNR, the Minnesota DNR, and the Wisconsin DNR.

## State-by-state license breakdown

### Ohio

A resident hunting license is $19, and you'll add a $31.20 either-sex deer permit or a $15 deer management permit (antlerless only) on top. Nonresidents pay $180.96 for the hunting license and $218.40 for the either-sex permit. Youth 17 and under pay $10 for the license and $16 for either deer permit. No draw required — buy online at [wildohio.gov](https://wildohio.gov) any time before you hunt.

### Michigan

Michigan uses a two-step system: every hunter buys a base license first, then adds a species-specific deer license. The resident base license is $11, and a single deer license is $20 — $31 total. Nonresidents pay a $151 base license plus the same $20 deer license, for $171. Youth ages 10–16 get a discounted $6 base license, and the deer license runs $20 for youth too (or $40 for a two-tag deer combo). Note: this is the current, confirmed 2026-27 fee schedule straight from Michigan's own deer regulations summary — a separate, unrelated 2027 budget proposal exists that would restructure fees for a future license year, but it hasn't taken effect.

### Indiana

A resident annual hunting license is $20. Single-season deer licenses (archery, firearm, or muzzleloader) run $39 each, or you can buy the Deer License Bundle for $91, which covers two antlerless tags and one antlered tag across every season. Nonresidents pay $90 for the hunting license and $240 per single-season deer license, or $550 for the full bundle. Everything is over the counter — no draw, no application window.

### Iowa

Residents pay $22 for the hunting license plus roughly $33 for a resident buck (any-sex) tag — the cheapest resident deer setup in this group. Nonresidents face a very different process: a $131 hunting license, a $15 habitat fee, and a mandatory $498 any-sex-plus-antlerless tag combination, for about $644 total. Nonresident tags are capped and awarded by lottery — the 2026 application window ran May 2 through June 7, with results and any excess-tag sales following in August. If you're planning a nonresident Iowa hunt for next season, mark that window early; it's the tightest deadline in this guide.

### Illinois

Illinois runs deer permits through a multi-round lottery for both residents and nonresidents, which makes it the hardest state in this group to give a single price. A resident hunting license is $12.50, and archery either-sex permits have run in the $17–$25.50 range depending on the lottery round and county. Nonresidents pay $57.75 for the license, with firearm either-sex permits landing around $300 after a successful draw (archery landowner permits run closer to $210). Illinois held three separate deer lotteries in 2026, with the last window running July 13 through August 21, and remaining county-specific permits go over the counter starting October 20. One detail specific to this site's coverage: Illinois prohibits centerfire rifles for deer statewide, but straight-wall cartridge rifles are permitted in a specific list of counties that changes annually — check the current list at [dnr.illinois.gov](https://dnr.illinois.gov) before you plan a rifle hunt there.

### Minnesota

A resident small-game license is $22, and the deer license — confirmed directly against the DNR's licensing catalog, and priced identically whether you hunt firearm, archery, or muzzleloader — is $34, for $56 total. Nonresidents pay $102 for the base license and $185 for the deer license, landing around $287. Youth 13-17 pay a flat $5 for a deer license regardless of residency or weapon type, and youth 10-12 hunt for free. Minnesota sells deer tags over the counter in most permit areas — no draw required for a standard license, though antlerless permits in lottery-designated deer permit areas require declaring your area and hitting the September lottery deadline (September 10 for 2026) to be automatically entered.

### Wisconsin

Confirmed directly against Wisconsin DNR's GoWild resident and nonresident fee pages: a resident deer license — gun, archery, or crossbow, all priced the same — is $24. Junior hunters (12-17) pay $20, and hunters under 12 pay $7. Nonresidents pay a flat $200 for a gun, archery, or crossbow deer license (a big correction from the $160-200 range floating around most aggregator sites — the real number is a clean $200 across the board), with a $24 rate for students and military and $7 for nonresidents under 12. A proposed fee increase (to $44 resident) has been floated for a future budget cycle but has not taken effect as of this writing.

## What else you should know

A few details that don't fit neatly into the price-and-dates breakdown above, but matter just as much once you're in the field:

- **Hunter education cutoff birthdates vary by state.** Ohio requires it for anyone born on or after Jan. 1, 1938 (functionally, everyone). Michigan's cutoff is Jan. 1, 1960. Indiana requires it for anyone born after Dec. 31, 1986. Iowa's cutoff is Jan. 1, 1972. Illinois requires it for hunters born on or after Jan. 1, 1980. Minnesota's cutoff is Dec. 31, 1979. Every state in this group also offers an apprentice license that lets a new hunter skip the course temporarily while hunting under a licensed mentor — useful if you're bringing someone new out this season and haven't gotten them certified yet.
- **Digital tagging is replacing paper tags in more states each year.** Michigan's eHarvest system now covers deer, turkey, bear, bobcat, otter, fisher, and marten — no paper tag required if you use the DNR Hunt Fish app. Ohio's HuntFish OH app handles game check for deer and turkey the same way. If you're used to carrying a paper tag, check whether your state has moved to digital before opening day.
- **Crossbow rules keep loosening.** Minnesota's legislature voted in spring 2026 to permanently remove the expiration date on crossbow use — any archery license now covers a crossbow for the entire season, for any hunter, no age or disability requirement. Wisconsin made a similar permanent change. Michigan allows crossbows through its full Lower Peninsula archery season, with narrower windows in the Upper Peninsula.
- **Nonresident landowner exemptions exist, but they're reciprocal and inconsistent.** Indiana, for example, exempts Iowa, Ohio, North Carolina, Virginia, and (partially) Wisconsin landowners from needing a license to hunt their own Indiana farmland — but explicitly does not extend that courtesy to landowners from Michigan, Illinois, or several other states. If you own ag ground across a state line, don't assume reciprocity; check the specific rule.
- **CWD carcass movement restrictions apply across most of this group.** Ohio, Michigan, Minnesota, and Wisconsin all have Chronic Wasting Disease surveillance or management zones with restrictions on moving whole carcasses out of the zone. If you're hunting away from home or processing game to bring back across a state line, check current CWD zone maps before you travel — this is a fast-changing area year to year.
- **License years don't line up across states.** Ohio and Minnesota run March 1 to the end of February. Indiana and Wisconsin run April 1 to March 31. Michigan's base license runs through March 31, but the deer season itself is framed Aug. 1 to July 31. If you're buying licenses for a multi-state trip, don't assume one purchase date logic applies everywhere.

## FAQ

**How much does a deer hunting license cost in Ohio?**
A resident hunting license is $19, plus a $31.20 either-sex deer permit — about $50 total. Nonresidents pay $180.96 for the license and $218.40 for the permit, about $399 total.

**How much does a deer hunting license cost in Michigan?**
Residents pay $11 for the base license plus $20 for a deer license, about $31 total. Nonresidents pay a $151 base license plus the same $20 deer license, about $171 total.

**How much does a deer hunting license cost in Indiana?**
A resident hunting license is $20, and a single-season deer license is $39 (or $91 for the full-season bundle). Nonresidents pay $90 for the license and $240 per deer license (or $550 for the bundle).

**When does gun deer season start in Wisconsin?**
Wisconsin's traditional 9-day gun season runs Nov. 21–29, 2026 — the Saturday before Thanksgiving through the Sunday after, as usual. Archery and crossbow season opens earlier, Sept. 12, 2026.

**Do I need to enter a lottery to hunt deer in these states?**
Ohio, Michigan, Indiana, Minnesota, and Wisconsin sell deer tags over the counter with no draw required. Iowa requires nonresidents to apply through a lottery (residents buy over the counter). Illinois runs a lottery for both residents and nonresidents.

**How much does a nonresident deer license cost in Wisconsin?**
A flat $200, whether you hunt with a gun, bow, or crossbow — confirmed directly from Wisconsin DNR's GoWild fee pages. Add a nonresident base license on top of that.

**Which of these states has the cheapest nonresident deer hunt?**
Michigan, at roughly $171 total for a base license and single deer tag — well below Wisconsin's $200 flat deer license, Iowa's roughly $644, or Illinois's roughly $358-plus after a successful draw.

## Bottom Line

These fees and dates change most years, and zone- or county-level exceptions can override anything general listed here. Bookmark your state's DNR licensing page, not a third-party aggregator, and check it fresh before every season — this guide is accurate as of August 2026, but treat it as a starting point, not the final word by the time you're at checkout.
