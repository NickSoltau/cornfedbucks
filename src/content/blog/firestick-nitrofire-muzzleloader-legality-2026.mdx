**FireStick and NitroFire muzzleloaders are legal during dedicated muzzleloader season in Ohio, Michigan, Indiana, Iowa, Wisconsin, and Minnesota for the 2026-27 season.** Indiana's legality currently rests on a fast-tracked interim rule — it's the law in the field now. The Natural Resources Commission took up permanent adoption at its September 15, 2026 meeting; official confirmation of the vote outcome isn't posted yet, though Indiana DNR's own equipment guidance page has already been updated to reflect the new breech-loading language. Even if adopted, the rule still needs Attorney General and Governor sign-off before taking effect. Illinois's status remains unconfirmed pending direct IDNR confirmation.

[FireStick](https://www.federalpremium.com/muzzleloading/firestick/) is Federal's breech-loaded, encapsulated propellant system built for the Traditions NitroFire and CVA Crossfire. It replaces loose or pelletized powder with a sealed, factory-measured capsule that loads from the breech instead of down the barrel — no damp powder ruining a hunt, no inconsistent charges, load and unload without firing the gun.

**Update, September 17, 2026: Since our last update in August, the Natural Resources Commission took up the permanent rule (312 IAC 9-3-3(d)) for final action at its September 15, 2026 meeting**, on schedule with the timeline DNR laid out after its May and July hearings. No official minutes or vote outcome have been posted yet. One signal worth noting: Indiana DNR's live equipment guidance page now reads "Powder may be loaded from the breech end" — the new proposed language, not the old muzzle-only wording — though that's a guidance-page update, not confirmation of a formal vote result on its own. Even if the Commission adopted the rule, it still needs Attorney General and Governor sign-off before it's final, so there's no effective date yet. We're checking every few days for official confirmation and will update this report the moment we have it.

## What FireStick Actually Is

The bullet still loads from the muzzle, same as any inline. The difference is the propellant charge — a sealed, factory-measured polymer capsule that loads from the breech instead of being poured or dropped down the barrel.

You still add a standard 209 primer yourself. Federal says the sealed charge keeps powder dry in any weather and gives shot-to-shot consistency that loose powder or pellets can't match.

The debate over whether that still counts as a "muzzleloader" in the traditional sense — powder and bullet both loaded from the muzzle — is exactly what's playing out in state rulemaking right now.

## Indiana: Legal This Season via Interim Rule, Permanent Vote Taken — Outcome Pending Confirmation

Indiana already had this fight once. In 2020, a rule change to allow FireStick-style muzzleloaders during muzzleloader season was proposed and rejected by the Natural Resources Commission after pushback from hunters who felt it gave an unfair edge over traditional muzzleloading equipment.

A new citizen petition restarted the process in 2025. DNR staff reviewed it, included questions in the 2024–25 Indiana Deer Management Survey, and came back in support. They noted no safety concerns, and pointed out that breech-loaded muzzleloaders wouldn't extend range beyond what's already legal for standard .50-caliber inlines.

DNR's survey asked people who currently hunt with a muzzleloader how the change would affect them: half said it wouldn't change their habits at all, 28% said they'd be unlikely to use the new equipment, and 22% said they'd likely use it.

Here's where it gets a little unusual — this change has been moving on two tracks at once:

- **The permanent track** folds it into a larger biennial rules package (312 IAC 9, LSA #26-99) alongside unrelated changes like bobcat season and armadillo classification. That package has now cleared two public comment periods, with the second closing after a public hearing on July 30, 2026. The Natural Resources Commission took this up for final adoption at its September 15, 2026 meeting — official minutes and the vote outcome aren't posted yet. You can track it yourself on the [Indiana NRC's rulemaking docket](https://www.in.gov/nrc/rules/rulemaking-docket/).
- **A separate interim rule** (LSA #26-202) fast-tracked the same change ahead of that vote. It received the Governor's approval, and its comment period has already closed.

That interim rule is why this is legal today, months ahead of the permanent vote. Indiana's [2026-2027 Hunting & Trapping Guide](https://www.in.gov/dnr/fish-and-wildlife/hunting-and-trapping/hunting-trapping-guide/) — the state's own current summary of the rules, updated August 18, 2026 — now lists breech-loading muzzleloaders as allowed during muzzleloader season.

**Practically speaking: breech-loading muzzleloaders like FireStick, NitroFire, and Crossfire are legal during Indiana's dedicated muzzleloader season for 2026-27, per the state's current official guide.** The Commission has now taken up that vote, though official confirmation of the outcome isn't posted yet — even once adopted, it still needs Attorney General and Governor sign-off before taking effect, so it wouldn't change what's legal in the field this year regardless. We'll update this report again the moment we have official confirmation.
