If you've spent any time in a muzzleloader-season deer camp the last few years, you've probably heard about FireStick — Federal's breech-loaded, encapsulated propellant system built for the Traditions NitroFire and CVA Crossfire. The pitch is simple: no damp powder ruining a hunt, no inconsistent charges, load and unload without firing the gun.

Right now, whether a **FireStick muzzleloader is legal** depends entirely on which state you hunt. It's not a straight-wall rifle and it's not a shotgun — it's a genuine next step in muzzleloader tech, and it's caught on fast. But legality is a state-by-state patchwork, and one of our core four is still working it out.

**This one's still moving, so consider this your standing update.** We're tracking the Indiana decision as it happens, and we'll come back and update this page the day it lands — no need to go digging through DNR meeting minutes yourself. That's what we're here for.

## What FireStick Actually Is

The bullet still loads from the muzzle, same as any inline. The difference is the propellant charge — a sealed, factory-measured polymer capsule that loads from the breech instead of being poured or dropped down the barrel.

You still add a standard 209 primer yourself. Federal says the sealed charge keeps powder dry in any weather and gives shot-to-shot consistency that loose powder or pellets can't match.

The debate over whether that still counts as a "muzzleloader" in the traditional sense — powder and bullet both loaded from the muzzle — is exactly what's playing out in state rulemaking right now.

## Where FireStick Muzzleloaders Are Legal Right Now

- **Ohio, Michigan, Iowa, Wisconsin, Minnesota** — legal for use during dedicated muzzleloader season.
- **Illinois** — reported as legal in past coverage, but we found conflicting information while researching this piece and haven't been able to verify current status against Illinois DNR's own regulations digest. Treat this as unconfirmed until Illinois hunters can confirm directly with IDNR.
- **Indiana** — not currently legal during muzzleloader season, though that's actively changing. Here's the story.

## Is Indiana About to Legalize FireStick Muzzleloaders?

Indiana already had this fight once. In 2020, a rule change to allow FireStick-style muzzleloaders during muzzleloader season was proposed and rejected by the Natural Resources Commission after pushback from hunters who felt it gave an unfair edge over traditional muzzleloading equipment.

A new citizen petition restarted the process in 2025. DNR staff reviewed it, included questions in the 2024–25 Indiana Deer Management Survey, and came back in support. They noted no safety concerns, and pointed out that breech-loaded muzzleloaders wouldn't extend range beyond what's already legal for standard .50-caliber inlines.

Of the roughly 16,000 hunters who answered the survey question, 33% favored allowing it, 19% opposed, and 44% were neutral.

The rule change has now cleared two public comment periods, with the second closing after a public hearing on July 30, 2026. The Natural Resources Commission still has to vote to formally adopt, reject, or modify it — and that vote happens at a full commission meeting, not on a rolling basis. Based on the commission's typical meeting cadence, expect that vote sometime this fall.

**Until that vote happens, FireStick-equipped muzzleloaders remain legal in Indiana only during firearms season, youth season, and reduction zones — not during the dedicated muzzleloader season.**

We'll update this report the moment the NRC votes.

## The Bigger Pattern

This is the same shape as the straight-wall cartridge expansion we've tracked for years now: a technology proves itself in the field, hunters push for it state by state, and agencies eventually catch up — usually slower than manufacturers would like, and occasionally after a false start.
