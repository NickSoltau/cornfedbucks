**As of 2026, Ohio, Michigan, Indiana, Iowa, Illinois, West Virginia, Maryland, Pennsylvania, Arkansas, Tennessee, and Delaware all mandate straight-wall cartridges for deer hunting in at least part of the state, though the required caliber, case shape, and zone vary — and Indiana, Iowa, and Michigan no longer require a straight-wall case at all. Wisconsin and Minnesota never had a case-shape mandate in the first place: both allow any centerfire rifle.** If you hunt anywhere near a straight-wall cartridge state, you already know the drill: check the regs every single year, because they change more than people expect. In the last two seasons alone, Indiana dropped its case-shape requirement entirely, Michigan erased its Lower Peninsula rifle line, and Tennessee added a brand-new straight-wall season from scratch. Meanwhile Ohio hasn't budged since 2014.

This guide breaks down which **straight-wall cartridge states** exist right now, what each one actually requires, and — because laws like these change every legislative session — links straight to the source so you can verify before you buy ammo or load up.

## The short version: what changed for 2026

Three states loosened their rules this year, and one added a brand-new one:

- **Indiana** no longer requires straight-wall case shape at all. Any centerfire rifle cartridge with a bullet diameter of .219" (5.56mm) or larger is legal, on public or private land.
- **Iowa** dropped its shape requirement too — rifles just need an expanding bullet .350"–.500" in diameter with 500+ ft-lbs of muzzle energy. Bottleneck cartridges that meet those numbers now qualify.
- **Michigan** eliminated its Limited Firearms Deer Zone in the Lower Peninsula for 2026, opening the door to "all legal firearms, including bottleneck cartridges," across the whole zone.
- **Tennessee** is the one going the other direction: a brand-new 7-day straight-wall season on private land only, Nov. 14–20, 2026.

Two states worth flagging that *aren't* part of the "loosened" story: **Wisconsin** and **Minnesota** never restricted deer rifles to straight-wall cases to begin with — both have allowed any centerfire rifle caliber for years. If you've seen either one grouped in with the SWC-mandate states, that's outdated.

Net effect: the "core four" SWC-mandate states most hunters think of (Ohio, Michigan, Indiana, Iowa) are down to really just **Ohio**. The other three have moved to performance-based standards or dropped the shape requirement altogether. That doesn't mean straight-wall cartridges got worse — a .350 Legend or .400 Legend is still a great choice in all four — it just means the *law* no longer forces the issue in three of them.

## Why these rules exist in the first place

Every one of these laws traces back to the same concern: population density and terrain. States with flat, open, heavily farmed ground and a lot of people living close to hunting land didn't want traditional bottleneck rifle cartridges in play, because they carry dangerous energy well past 400–500 yards. A straight-wall cartridge sheds velocity fast enough that its practical range tops out around 200 yards for most loads — plenty for row-crop whitetail, but a lot less likely to cause a problem if a shot goes long.

That's also why you're seeing the recent split in approach. Indiana, Iowa, and Michigan moved to performance-based standards (diameter and energy) instead of policing case shape, because the actual safety goal — limiting range — can be met without dictating whether the brass tapers. Wisconsin and Minnesota reached a similar place from a different direction: both simply never wrote a case-shape rule into their deer hunting regs, relying instead on minimum-caliber and expanding-bullet requirements. Tennessee, on the other hand, is opening a brand-new door and is being conservative about it: private land, single-shot, one narrow week.

## What this means if you're picking a cartridge

If you hunt Ohio, West Virginia, Maryland, Illinois, Delaware, Pennsylvania's special zones, Arkansas's alternative season, or Tennessee's new week, the shape of your cartridge still matters — pick something that's genuinely straight-wall. If you hunt Indiana, Iowa, Michigan's former Limited Firearms Zone, Wisconsin, or Minnesota, you have more options — any qualifying centerfire rifle, straight-wall or bottleneck — but a straight-wall cartridge like the .350 Legend or .400 Legend is still a smart, low-recoil choice for realistic row-crop shot distances either way.

## FAQ

**Do all straight-wall cartridge states require the same caliber?**
No. Minimum caliber requirements range from .30 (Arkansas) to .357 (Ohio, West Virginia, Maryland, Pennsylvania), and some states use diameter-and-energy standards instead of a caliber floor (Iowa). Always check your specific state.

**Can I hunt with a bottleneck cartridge in Indiana, Iowa, or Michigan now?**
Yes, as of the 2025–26 and 2026 seasons, all three have dropped their straight-wall shape requirement in the areas that used to mandate it, though other rules (diameter, energy, zone) may still apply. Confirm current-year specifics with each state's DNR.

**Is Wisconsin or Minnesota a straight-wall cartridge state?**
No — this is a common mix-up. Neither state has ever restricted deer rifles to straight-wall case shape. Wisconsin allows any centerfire rifle .22 caliber or larger statewide, and Minnesota allows any centerfire rifle .220 caliber or larger loaded with expanding-bullet ammunition. Both simply use caliber and bullet-type rules instead of a case-shape mandate.

**Is Tennessee a straight-wall cartridge state?**
As of 2026, yes — but narrowly. Tennessee allows single-shot straight-wall rifles .35 caliber or larger on private land only, during a 7-day window in November tied to muzzleloader season.

**How often do these regulations change?**
Often enough to check every year. Indiana, Iowa, Michigan, and Tennessee all changed their rules within the last two seasons. Bookmark your state DNR's hunting regulations page and check it before each season.
