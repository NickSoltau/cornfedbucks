## The Claim

Deer are color-blind. They can't see blaze orange, so wearing it costs you nothing in the stand.

## Can Deer See Blaze Orange? Verdict: Mostly Legit

Break the claim into two pieces, because they don't get the same grade.

**"Deer are color-blind" is a Legend.** Deer do see color. They just see fewer colors than you do.

In a 1994 study in the *Journal of Comparative Physiology A*, Gerald Jacobs and his team measured the eyes of white-tailed deer and fallow deer. They found two kinds of color-sensing cells, called cones. One is tuned to short wavelengths, around 450 to 460 nanometers, which is blue. The other peaks around 537 nanometers, in the green-yellow range. People have a third cone that handles long wavelengths. Deer don't. The researchers said this gives deer the basis for two-color, or dichromatic, vision.

What does that mean for orange? Jacobs and his colleague Jay Neitz concluded that deer can tell blue from yellow, but they lack what they'd need to tell apart colors in the yellow-green-to-red range. Orange and red are in that range.

Here's a way to think about it. A person with red-green color blindness still sees the car coming down the road. They just can't tell if the paint is red or green. A deer looking at your vest is in a similar spot: it sees *something* there, but the color isn't the giveaway.

**"Orange doesn't spook deer" is Legit.** A 2014 University of Georgia study by Bradley Cohen and others trained seven deer to pick out lit LED lights. The deer were more sensitive to short wavelengths and less sensitive to long ones, with some sensitivity to ultraviolet. Their eyes look built for dawn and dusk, and for spotting predators along the horizon.

Wildlife agencies in our region say the same thing. Iowa's DNR told hunters that [movement is what really tips deer off, not orange](https://www.iowadnr.gov/news-release/2017-11-29/why-doesnt-blaze-orange-give-me-away). Michigan's DNR says [hunter orange does not deter deer](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/about/newsroom/releases/2026/11/04/10-tips-for-a-safe-and-successful-firearm-deer-hunting-season).

## The Fine Print

- **"Can't tell it's orange" is not "can't see you."** Deer still see a shape, an outline, and anything that moves. The researchers' own advice is that holding still matters more than camo detail or color.
- **Deer see blue and some UV better than we do.** The UGA team told hunters not to wash camo in detergents with brightening agents, because those can add short-wavelength light deer may notice. They didn't claim to have measured whether that ruins a hunt. Iowa's DNR gives the same advice about brighteners.
- **We didn't find a field study of wild whitetails reacting to orange versus camo.** The eye-structure and lab work is solid. The "orange is safe in the stand" part rests on that work plus what the DNRs say. It's strong, but it isn't a test on Midwest bean-field deer.
- **Be careful with the "deer can't see orange at all" line.** The Jacobs abstract doesn't speak to orange directly. The orange claim comes from what the two-cone setup implies, which is why we word it as "not as orange" and not "invisible."

## Why You're Wearing It Anyway

The vest isn't for the deer. It's for the other hunter.

A North Carolina study covered 68 firearm hunting deaths from 1983 to 1992. In 22 percent of the incidents, the victim was mistaken for game. After the state's 1987-88 hunter orange law, the authors found a statistically significant drop in hunters killed after being mistaken for game.

On ag ground, sightlines are long and more hunters are carrying rifles. Iowa's DNR said this week that more hunters are choosing rifles, and rifles carry much farther, so blaze orange matters more.

## What to Do With This

- **Check your state's row in the table before you leave the truck.** A hat alone works in only two of the seven states, Michigan and Indiana. Camo orange counts in Ohio, Michigan, Minnesota and Wisconsin, but not in Iowa or Illinois, and Indiana's rule says solid.
- **Leave the orange on once you're in the stand.** Indiana's rule says so outright, and Ohio's covers the full legal hunting day.
- **Put camo under the vest.** That's the Iowa DNR's own advice for staying hidden from deer.
- **Don't skyline yourself.** The same Iowa DNR page says to stand in front of or behind something big, like a tree or brush, so your outline breaks up. On a field edge, that's a fence corner or a hedgerow, not a bare ridge. See [Building a Preseason Wind Chart](/blog/building-a-preseason-wind-chart/) for the wind side of staying hidden.
- **Check your orange for fade.** Iowa's DNR asked hunters this week to make sure gear still fits and hasn't faded to the point of being ineffective. The vest is there so another hunter can see you.
- **Rifle hunters on open ground: wear more than the minimum.** See [Standing vs. Cut Corn: Deer Hunting With a Rifle](/blog/standing-vs-cut-corn-rifle-hunting/) for how those sightlines play out.

## FAQ: Deer, Color Vision, and Blaze Orange

### Can deer see blaze orange?

Not as orange. Research on white-tailed deer found two kinds of color cones, so deer can tell blue from yellow but can't separate colors from yellow-green to red the way people do. They can still see a shape and any movement, so a vest doesn't make you invisible.

### Do deer see color at all?

Yes. A 1994 study found white-tailed deer have two types of color-sensing cones. A 2014 University of Georgia study also found they're more sensitive to short wavelengths like blue, with some sensitivity to ultraviolet.

### Does blaze orange spook deer?

Wildlife agencies say no. Michigan's DNR says hunter orange does not deter deer, and Iowa's DNR says movement is what tips deer off, not orange. Sitting still matters more than what you're wearing.

### Do I have to wear blaze orange if deer can't see it?

Yes. The rule is about other hunters, not deer. All seven Corn Belt states we cover require some form of blaze orange during firearm deer seasons, and the details differ by state, so check your state's current rule.

## The Bottom Line

Call it Mostly Legit. Deer aren't color-blind, but orange isn't what gives you away. Movement and outline are. Wear the vest for the guy across the field, stay still for the deer in it, and check your state's row in the table before you go.
