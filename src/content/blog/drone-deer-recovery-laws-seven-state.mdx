**Drone deer recovery is written into the rules in two of our seven Corn Belt states, Ohio and Indiana, and only with strict conditions. Michigan, Illinois, and Minnesota say no, even to find a deer you already shot. Wisconsin decides case by case, and Iowa's law doesn't mention drones at all.** If you're thinking about sending a drone up to find a downed buck this season, the answer depends on which side of the state line you're standing on.

Most hunting laws were written when "aircraft" meant a plane. Drones landed in the middle of that, and states have answered in different ways. One state went all the way to the U.S. Supreme Court over it. So here's where each state stands, based on the statute, rule, or DNR statement itself. We last checked everything on October 12, 2026.

This is a living report. Several bills could change the picture, and we'll update it when they do. It's also not legal advice. If you're unsure, call your state's conservation officer line before you fly.

## Ohio: Yes, With Two Conditions

Ohio is the clearest yes. Ohio Administrative Code 1501:31-15-02(B) bans hunting "by means, aid, or use of any aircraft, including aerial drones." Then it makes one exception: "Aerial drones may be used to aid in the recovery of a deer after the taking has occurred."

There are two conditions. Nobody involved in the recovery can be "employing a hunting device at the time." And "if said deer is found to be still alive, the use of the drone will be discontinued before hunting can resume."

The exception covers deer only. The rule says "employing" a hunting device and doesn't explain it in the part we read, so if you're not sure whether carrying your bow counts, ask an ODNR wildlife officer first. For season dates and licenses, see our [Ohio deer season dates guide](/ohio-deer-season-dates/).

## Indiana: Yes for Recovery, No for Scouting

Indiana's law, IC 14-22-6-16, does two things.

First, it bans using a drone to "search for, scout, locate, or detect" a wild animal as an aid to taking it. That ban starts 14 days before the season for that animal and runs through the last legal hunting hour of the season's last day. For deer, that means no scouting flights in the two weeks before opening day.

Second, it says you may legally fly a drone with infrared abilities to locate and recover a "legally taken" wild animal. That does not let you onto land you don't have permission to be on. The text we read doesn't say what happens if the animal is wounded but still alive, so don't treat it as a green light to track a live deer. Indiana last changed this section in 2024. See our [Indiana deer season dates guide](/indiana-deer-season-dates/).

## Michigan: No, and the Courts Agree

Michigan is a hard no. MCL 324.40111c(2) says an individual "shall not take game or fish using an unmanned vehicle or unmanned device that uses aerodynamic forces to achieve flight." Subsection (2) has no recovery exception.

A drone recovery company, Drone Deer Recovery Media, sued, arguing the law violated the First Amendment as applied to its business of using drones to help hunters find downed deer. The court's own opinion notes that Michigan DNR guidance treats attempting to locate or recover dead or wounded game as "take." The Pacific Legal Foundation, which represented the company, says the DNR told the founder the ban applies "even locating downed game after the hunter has stored his weapons."

The company lost. The Sixth Circuit upheld the law in July 2025 in *Yoder v. Bowen*, and denied a rehearing in October 2025. The U.S. Supreme Court declined to take the case on June 22, 2026. In a statement on the rehearing denial, Judge Bush described the law as one that "forbids employing drones to obtain and deliver the location of felled game." In Michigan, plan on finding your deer without a drone. See our [Michigan deer season dates guide](/michigan-deer-season-dates/).

## Illinois: No, Per IDNR, and a Bill Hasn't Moved

The Illinois Department of Natural Resources says it plainly. In a November 26, 2025 release, IDNR wrote: "The use of unmanned aircraft, or drones, for any aspect of hunting or recovery of wildlife is unlawful in Illinois." The release cites 520 ILCS 5/2.33(i) and 520 ILCS 5/1.2o and warns of enforcement, including seizure and forfeiture of the equipment.

A bill would change that. HB 2740 would add an exception to 2.33(i) "for the tracking of wounded animals in the course of hunting." It was filed in February 2025 and sent back to the Rules Committee on March 21, 2025. It has not passed either chamber, so it's pending, not law. See our [Illinois deer season dates guide](/illinois-deer-season-dates/).

## Minnesota: No, Per the DNR

Minnesota DNR enforcement guidance says "it is not legal to use drones to take big or small game," and that taking "includes using drones to recover dead or wounded game." It adds that "taking includes assisting in the take."

Two bills, HF 1301 and SF 741, would allow drones to help locate and recover dead big game. HF 1301 was introduced in February 2025, and its bill page shows no committee passage. SF 741 is still in committee. Neither is law as far as the Legislature's bill pages show. See our [Minnesota deer season dates guide](/minnesota-deer-season-dates/).

## Wisconsin: A Warden Decides

Wisconsin DNR says "aircraft are not allowed to be used for hunting" (s. 29.307, Wis. Stats., and NR 10.07(1)(a), Wis. Adm. Code), and it counts a drone as an aircraft. There's no yes or no for recovery. Wardens decide case by case.

According to the DNR, they look at things like these:

- Whether you made a reasonable effort to find the animal without a drone first
- Whether you can give a sound reason for believing the animal is dead
- Whether anyone present has a firearm, bow, or other legal weapon
- Whether the drone stays within the operator's line of sight
- Whether the pilot is a third party with no stake beyond helping find the animal

The DNR also warns that a hunter who shot the animal and then flies the drone himself risks a hunting-with-aircraft violation. Those factors are not safe harbors. See our [Wisconsin deer season dates guide](/wisconsin-deer-season-dates/).

## Iowa: The Gray Area

Iowa is the one that's truly unclear. Iowa Code 481A.120 bars intentionally killing, wounding, or pursuing an animal "from or with an aircraft in flight," or from a snowmobile. It doesn't mention drones, and it doesn't address finding a downed animal. We couldn't find a published Iowa DNR statement either way.

A bill to allow retrieving wounded deer with a registered drone, HF 2112, got a favorable House subcommittee recommendation in February 2026. We found no sign it went further or became law. Until Iowa spells it out, call Iowa DNR law enforcement before you fly. See our [Iowa deer season dates guide](/iowa-deer-season-dates/).

## What About Hiring a Drone Recovery Pilot?

Hiring someone else to fly doesn't get around the state rules. As written, they apply to whoever is in the air, and in some states to anyone who helps.

- **Michigan:** The *Yoder* case was a business that did exactly this, and it lost.
- **Illinois and Minnesota:** IDNR says drones for "any aspect of hunting or recovery of wildlife" are unlawful. Minnesota DNR guidance says "taking includes assisting in the take." Neither leaves room for a hired pilot.
- **Wisconsin:** Wardens weigh whether the operator is a third party with no stake beyond helping find the animal. That's one factor, not a safe harbor.
- **Ohio:** The condition covers "no one involved in the recovery," so it applies to the pilot and the hunter alike.
- **Indiana:** The statute says "a person may" fly an infrared drone to recover a legally taken animal. We didn't see it treat an owner and a hired operator differently.
- **Iowa:** Unclear for everyone, hired or not.

That's our reading of the statute and agency wording. None of these states has told us how it treats a paid pilot, so if you plan to hire one, ask the state first.

### What to Look For in a Recovery Pilot

If your state allows it, ask these before you book anyone:

- **Do they know your state's rule?** A pilot who says "it's fine everywhere" hasn't read the law. Ask them to point to it.
- **Are they certified?** The FAA says you can fly a drone under 55 pounds for work or business by following Part 107, which requires a Remote Pilot Certificate. Ask to see it.
- **Do they have your landowner's permission in hand?** Indiana's rule says a recovery flight doesn't let anyone onto land without it. Get it in writing before the flight.
- **Do they have an infrared drone, if your state requires one?** Indiana's rule names infrared.
- **Will they stop if the deer is alive?** In Ohio, the drone use has to end before hunting resumes. Agree on that up front.
- **What is the fee, and what happens if they find nothing?** Get both in writing.

## What About Federal Law?

The Airborne Hunting Act (16 U.S.C. 742j-1) bars shooting at or harassing wildlife from an aircraft, and it defines aircraft as "any contrivance used for flight in the air." It doesn't mention drones or recovery, so the state rules above are what you plan around. You also have to follow FAA rules, as Wisconsin's DNR notes.

## Before You Fly

- **Check your state first.** The same flight can be allowed in Ohio and a violation in Michigan.
- **Get landowner permission.** Indiana's rule says so outright, and it's a good habit everywhere.
- **Don't hunt and fly at once.** Ohio's condition is a good rule of thumb: nobody in the recovery should be using a weapon while the drone is up.
- **Know your other options.** A drone isn't the only way to find a deer. See our notes on [tracking a deer after the shot](/blog/shot-placement-tracking-straight-wall-rifle/).

## FAQ: Drone Deer Recovery Laws

### Is drone deer recovery legal in the Corn Belt?

It depends on the state. Ohio and Indiana write a recovery exception into their rules, with conditions. Michigan, Illinois, and Minnesota say no. Wisconsin decides case by case, and Iowa's law doesn't mention drones.

### Is it legal to use a drone to find a deer in Ohio?

Yes, after the deer has been taken. Ohio Administrative Code 1501:31-15-02(B) allows it as long as no one involved in the recovery is employing a hunting device. If the deer is still alive, you must stop using the drone before hunting resumes.

### Can I scout deer with a drone in Indiana?

No, not in the 14 days before the season or during it. IC 14-22-6-16 bans using a drone to search for, scout, locate, or detect a wild animal as an aid to taking it during that window.

### Is drone deer recovery legal in Michigan?

No. MCL 324.40111c(2) bars taking game using a drone. A federal court upheld the law as applied to a drone recovery company, and the U.S. Supreme Court declined to review the case on June 22, 2026.

### Is drone deer recovery legal in Illinois?

No, according to the Illinois DNR. Its November 2025 release says using drones for any aspect of hunting or recovery of wildlife is unlawful. A bill to allow tracking wounded animals, HB 2740, has not passed.

### Is drone deer recovery legal in Wisconsin?

It depends. Wisconsin bans aircraft for hunting and counts drones as aircraft, but its DNR says wardens weigh the facts of each recovery. A hunter flying the drone himself after shooting the animal risks a violation.

### Is drone deer recovery legal in Minnesota?

No, according to the Minnesota DNR. Its enforcement guidance says it is not legal to use drones to take big or small game, and that includes using drones to recover dead or wounded game. Two bills to change that, HF 1301 and SF 741, have not passed.

### Is drone deer recovery legal in Iowa?

It's unclear. Iowa Code 481A.120 bars hunting "from or with an aircraft in flight" but never mentions drones, and we found no Iowa DNR statement on recovery. Call Iowa DNR law enforcement before you fly.

### Can I hire someone to find my deer with a drone?

Not safely without checking your state first. Michigan, Illinois, and Minnesota say no, and the rules in other states apply to anyone involved in the recovery. Wisconsin treats a third-party pilot as one factor wardens weigh, not as permission.
