The best deer call for rut hunting an ag field is one you can run soft and loud: soft for a buck you can see, loud for one across the bean stubble. Pair it with a rattling device and you can build the whole rut sequence for about $55. This guide gives you three Good/Better/Best picks, and it's honest about the research up front. We found a real study on rattling. We didn't find one on grunt calls or bleats, so we'll mark where we're reasoning from the field.

Timing is the first thing to get right. In the one controlled study we found, which covered rattling, response was greatest in the rut. If you want the movement side of this, start with [when the rut starts](/blog/when-does-the-rut-start/) and [how bucks search for does](/blog/rut-straight-line-search/). This one is about what's on the lanyard.

## What an Open Field Changes About Calling

This section is our reading of the situation, plus what the experts we found say about open ground.

- **Deer can see you.** In timber, a buck walks in on a call and finds cover. In a field he gets a long look at your stand or blind. Bowhunter magazine's calling guide says the top reason bucks ignore a call is a hunter sitting in the open with nothing around him. The buck looks, sees nothing, and keeps walking.
- **Use cover.** Zach Ferenbaugh, one of the experts Outdoor Life interviewed, prefers thick vegetation between himself and the route he wants the deer to take. A fence line, a brush pile, or a standing-crop edge does the same job on a farm.
- **Call to deer you can see.** Dan Infalt, another of those experts, calls mostly to deer he can see that are just out of range. He saves blind calling for the golden hour, when deer are likely to be moving anyway.
- **Range is about 100 yards.** Outdoor Life's FAQ says deer can usually hear a grunt about 100 yards away in normal conditions, with terrain and wind changing that. That fits how most of us hunt ag ground, where shots run inside 150 yards. Mark Drury says he'll call to a deer 100 to 125 yards out, and says a call can stop a buck at the far end of a field.
- **The wind still wins.** In the Texas rattling study below, 67 of the 111 responding bucks (60 percent) were first seen downwind of the stand. Set up so the wind covers the side he's likely to circle.

## What One Study Found About Rattling

The study we found is Hellickson, Miller, Marchinton, DeYoung, and Hall (2009), "Behavioral Responses of Male White-tailed Deer to Antler Rattling," in the *Proceedings of the Southeastern Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies* (63:7-10). The team watched 111 male deer respond to four rattling sequences, performed 171 times from 1992 to 1995.

- **Loud beat quiet.** Loud rattling attracted almost three times as many males as quiet rattling.
- **Length didn't matter.** Short and long sessions drew about the same number of bucks.
- **Timing mattered.** Response was greatest in the rut and lowest in the pre-rut. Most responses came during the first 10-minute segment.
- **Age changed the timing.** Young males responded most in the pre-rut and middle-aged males in the rut. Males 5½ and older responded most after the rut and less during it, which the authors thought was because they were busy courting does.
- **Mornings won.** About half the responses, 60 of 111, came in morning sessions, per Hellickson's own write-up.
- **Bucks didn't learn to ignore it.** The males apparently didn't learn to avoid rattling.

**The fine print:** This was southern Texas, mostly on a wildlife refuge with a balanced buck-to-doe ratio and bucks of all ages. It wasn't a Corn Belt farm. It counted how deer responded, not how many ended up in a shooting lane. And it covered rattling only. The "loud wins" finding fits an open field, where sound has to travel, but that's our reading, not a result.

## How to Pick

- **Good** if this is your first call and you want one tube that does grunts and bleats.
- **Better** if you want a wide volume range and a snort-wheeze for a buck that's moving off.
- **Best** if you want to run the full sequence, grunt, wheeze, then rattle, and you've got a grunt call already.

Whatever you buy, practice it away from the field first. A call that sounds fake at 60 yards sounds worse at 30. And don't overdo it. Hellickson's team found most responses came in the first 10 minutes, so give it a short sequence, then be quiet and watch the downwind side.

## FAQ: Deer Calls for Rut Hunting

### Do deer calls work in open ag fields?

They can, with cover. Deer in a field can see you, so a call without anything to hide behind can pull a buck's head up and nothing else. Set up on a fence line, brush, or crop edge, and call to deer you can see. Outdoor Life's FAQ puts a grunt's range at about 100 yards in normal conditions.

### When is the best time to use a deer call?

The rut. The only controlled study we found, on rattling in Texas, had the highest response in the rut and the lowest in the pre-rut. We didn't find a study on grunt calls, so we can't say the same is proven for them.

### Should I rattle loud or soft?

Loud, if you're in the open. In that Texas study, loud rattling attracted almost three times as many males as quiet rattling, and the length of the session didn't change the result. Keep sessions short, since most responses came in the first 10 minutes.

## The Bottom Line

A call won't make up for a bad set-up. In an open field, the stand, the cover, and the wind do most of the work, and the call finishes the job. Buy one grunt call you can run soft and loud, add a rattler if you want the full sequence, and keep the sessions short.
